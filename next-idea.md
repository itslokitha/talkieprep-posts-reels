## 1. « Je suis d'accord. » Et après ? ⭐ RECOMMENDED

- **Exam(s) and language:** TCF Canada and TEF Canada, expression orale (French) — fully in French.
  Both ask you to give a point of view and defend it out loud.
- **Why it will land:** Canada PR candidates hear the question, say « Je suis d'accord, c'est
  important », and stop. The French is fine; the argument is missing. The fear: "I understood
  everything and still had nothing to say." The fix is a 3-step route: avis → raison → exemple
  (then « donc » to close).
- **Accuracy notes:** Don't state task numbers, timings or score bands unless verified first.
  Keep it to "give and defend your opinion", which both exams ask for. No promised NCLC levels.
- **Day 2 post — angle A (format: transcript teardown carousel, in French):**
  A practice transcript of a 2-line opinion ("Je suis d'accord. C'est important pour la
  société.") marked in coral: no reason, no example, vague word. Slide by slide, rebuild it with
  avis · parce que · par exemple · donc, and show the longer, specific version.
  Hook: "Votre français est bon. / **Votre argument manque.**"
- **Day 3 reel — angle + hook:** timer reel on a TalkiePrep-style screen. The speaker says one
  sentence, then silence while the timer runs; the 3 chips (Avis · Raison · Exemple) drop in;
  the rerun keeps going.
  Hook (0–2s): « Je suis d'accord. » … et après ? ⏱️
- **Day 4 post — angle B (format: save-this phrase bank / fill-in template, in French):**
  One slide per step with 3 starter phrases each (« À mon avis… », « La raison principale,
  c'est que… », « Par exemple, dans mon pays… », « Donc, pour moi… »), ending with a blank
  avis/raison/exemple grid to screenshot. Different from Day 2 (template vs teardown).

## 2. It's not your accent. It's word stress.

- **Exam(s) and language:** IELTS Speaking (English); also useful for TOEFL and Cambridge.
- **Why it will land:** People spend months on "neutralising" their accent. Listeners forgive
  accents; they get lost when the stress lands on the wrong syllable (PHOtograph / phoTOgraphy /
  photoGRAPHic, deVElopment, eCOnomy). Pronunciation is one of the four things assessed.
- **Day 2 post — angle A (format: myth-bust carousel):** "Myth: I need a native accent" →
  one word family per slide with the stressed syllable in teal, the common miss in coral.
- **Day 3 reel — hook:** "Same word. Twice. Which one did you hear? 🔊" (on-screen text carries
  the stress marks so it works silent).
- **Day 4 post — angle B (format: quiz "1, 2 or 3?"):** "Where's the stress?" three options per
  word, answers on the next slide.

## 3. „Ja, gute Idee." ×10 is not a conversation.

- **Exam(s) and language:** Goethe-Zertifikat B1, Sprechen — the "plan something together"
  task (German).
- **Why it will land:** Nervous candidates agree with everything so they don't make mistakes.
  The task is to plan together: suggest, react, disagree politely, decide. Agreeing ten times
  leaves you nothing to show.
- **Day 2 post — angle A (format: A vs B "which scores higher"):** the same planning dialogue
  twice: A = "Ja, gute Idee" on loop; B = "Wie wäre es mit…?", "Das finde ich gut, aber…",
  "Dann machen wir es so."
- **Day 3 reel — hook:** "Ja. Ja. Gute Idee. Ja. 💀" — a chat-style mock-up filling with yeses.
- **Day 4 post — angle B (format: "Stop saying X / say this instead" phrase upgrades).**

## 4. Mente en blanco? Di esto.

- **Exam(s) and language:** DELE A2–B2 (Spanish).
- **Why it will land:** The blank moment mid-answer in Spanish, when English thinking words
  ("um", "like") leak out. Spanish has its own ways to buy 3 seconds: « Pues… a ver… »,
  « ¿Cómo lo diría? », « Es una buena pregunta… », « Lo que quiero decir es que… ».
- **Day 2 post — angle A (format: phrases for when your mind goes blank):** one phrase per
  slide, when to use it, and a short example.
- **Day 3 reel — hook:** "4 seconds of silence and counting ⏱️" — live transcript freezes, a
  phrase card saves it.
- **Day 4 post — angle B (format: transcript teardown):** a practice transcript with English
  fillers highlighted in coral, then the same answer with Spanish bridges in teal.

## 5. The first time you hear yourself answer shouldn't be test day.

- **Exam(s) and language:** All exams; language chips English · Français · Español · Deutsch.
- **Why it will land:** Most people prepare by reading, writing answers and rehearsing in their
  head. Then, in the exam, they hear their own voice under a timer for the first time and
  their mind goes blank. Nerves come from the unfamiliar, so make it familiar.
- **Day 2 post — angle A (format: myth-bust list):** "Practising in your head counts" ✗,
  "Writing answers = speaking practice" ✗, "I'll be fine on the day" ✗ — each with the fix.
- **Day 3 reel — hook:** "Speaking exam simulator. Mic ready? 🎙️" — a full-run mock-up:
  question, timer, live practice transcript.
- **Day 4 post — angle B (format: 7-day plan):** a week of short spoken runs, building to one
  full timed session before test day.
