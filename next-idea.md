### 1. You don't run out of ideas. You run out of structure. ⭐ RECOMMENDED

- **Exam(s) and language:** IELTS Speaking Part 2 (English)
- **Why it will land:** The single most common Part 2 fear: you speak for 50 seconds, then
  silence while the examiner waits. People blame vocabulary or "not having ideas". The real

### 2. "Euh…" n'est pas votre problème. Le silence non plus.

- **Exam(s) and language:** DELF B1/B2, TCF, TEF (French) — fully in French
- **Why it will land:** Learners panic about every "euh" and freeze trying to avoid it, which
  makes the pauses longer. Natural French has its own thinking words; using them sounds fluent,
  not hesitant.
- **Day 2 post — angle A (format: "Stop saying X / dites plutôt" upgrades):**
  "Euh… euh…" → "Alors, disons que…", "Comment dire…", "En fait, ce qui compte, c'est…".
  One swap per slide, with when to use each.
- **Day 3 reel — hook:** "Votre transcription ne ment pas 💀" — TalkiePrep transcript with
  coral highlights on every "euh", then the rerun with bridge phrases.
- **Day 4 post — angle B (format: A vs B "laquelle sonne plus naturelle ?"):** two versions of
  the same answer side by side; comment A ou B.

### 3. "Realizar" doesn't mean "realise". It's costing you in DELE.

- **Exam(s) and language:** DELE A2–B2 (Spanish), for English speakers
- **Why it will land:** False friends sound confident and are wrong. Learners often don't know
  they are making them, so they repeat them through the whole exam.
- **Day 2 post — angle A (format: myth-bust list / red pen):** realizar ≠ realise (darse cuenta),
  actual ≠ actual (real), embarazada ≠ embarrassed (avergonzado/a), carpeta ≠ carpet (alfombra),
  sensible ≠ sensible (sensato/a). Coral strikethrough, teal fix.
- **Day 3 reel — hook:** "You just told the examiner you're pregnant 💀"
- **Day 4 post — angle B (format: quiz "1, 2 or 3?"):** pick the right word in context.

### 4. "Weil ich habe…" Your verb is in the wrong place.

- **Exam(s) and language:** Goethe-Zertifikat A2–B1 (German)
- **Why it will land:** Learners hear "weil" + main-clause order in everyday speech and copy it.
  In an exam, the safe choice is verb-final after weil/dass/wenn. A small, fixable habit.
- **Day 2 post — angle A (format: A vs B "which scores higher"):**
  "…weil ich habe keine Zeit" vs "…weil ich keine Zeit habe", then dass and wenn.
- **Day 3 reel — hook:** "Same sentence. Twice. Which one is exam-safe? 🔊 SOUND ON"
- **Day 4 post — angle B (format: "red pen" marked answer):** a short spoken answer with
  three word-order slips marked and fixed.
